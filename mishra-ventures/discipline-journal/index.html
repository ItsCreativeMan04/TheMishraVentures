Trading Research Platform

A journal that grades
process, not profit.

Most trading journals record what you earned. That teaches the wrong lesson, because a rule you broke can still pay — and then you do it again. This one checks whether you followed your own rules, and treats a profitable rule-break as a warning.

“Lucky profit is bad, then discipline loss/profit.” — the line that started this tool

01The problem it solves

Two habits, both invisible in a normal P&L: entering before the setup actually appears, and taking one more trade than planned. Neither shows up in a profit number — a broken rule that made money looks identical to a good trade.

Can't wait for the setup

Entering in open air because the screen is boring. The journal checks the entry price against the zones that were actually rated that morning.

Overtrading without noticing

The limit is two. The third trade gets flagged as it is logged, before the day is over.

Learning from luck

The most expensive habit. Profit from a broken rule reinforces the break. It gets its own box below.

03Four fields typed, the rest looked up

Manual journals die because they're work. This one asks for direction, entry price, entry time and exit. Everything used for grading is read from files the platform already writes each night.

Was it inside a rated zone?

The entry price is checked against the demand/supply zones that were in force at that session's open — archived before the day traded, so it can't be rewritten afterwards.

Did the system confirm it?

Looks for a trap, sweep-reclaim or structure-shift alert within ±15 minutes of the entry.

Which trade of the day?

04Three verdicts, not two

This is the design decision the tool most depends on being right.

PASS

Entry was inside a rated zone, a confirmation fired nearby, and you were within your trade limit.

FAIL

Positive evidence of a break — open-air entry, no confirmation, or past the limit.

UNKNOWN

The monitoring agent wasn't running, so there's no record either way. Never counted against you.

Absent evidence is not a rule break. The monitoring agent only observed about 3 of 23 trading days in its first month. If a missing alert record counted as a failure, the report would call the trader undisciplined on days he may have traded perfectly — and he'd “correct” behaviour that was never wrong. That turns an uptime problem into an invented psychological one, so it's pinned by its own tests.

06What it refuses to grade

Disagreeing with the system

Discretion is allowed. The report shows separately whether overrides paid off — that's information, not a rule.

Profit

Grading on profit is what makes journals lie. The tool tracks it, reports it, and never lets it decide whether a day was good.

Days you sat out

Deliberately not trading counts as a disciplined day. The behaviour being built has to leave a trace, or the report only ever shows failures.
